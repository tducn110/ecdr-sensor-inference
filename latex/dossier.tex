\section{Working research question}
\begin{quote}
Can structured, event-conditioned class-pair bounds certify that a neural backbone preserves its decision on temporally correlated inputs more often than global Lipschitz bounds, while remaining cheap enough to produce net inference savings?
\end{quote}
Success = better Pareto frontier: tighter certificate + higher certified reuse + same backbone decision + lower total cost. No hard 50\% latency target.

Scope: streaming windows $x_t$ (HAPT: 6 channels $\times$ 128 samples, 50\,Hz); small verifier-friendly 1D-CNN (24,428 params iff bias everywhere, no BN, $K{=}12$; $K{=}6 \Rightarrow 24{,}038$; needs \texttt{scripts/count\_params.py} in G2). The certificate proves reuse has the \textbf{same prediction} as the backbone at a trusted anchor---it does \textbf{not} prove correctness vs.\ ground truth. Agreement and Accuracy are separate metrics.

\section{Core math (from roadmap, to freeze after G1)}
Anchor $x^r$ with $a = \arg\max_k f_k(x^r)$, margin $g_{aj}(x) = f_a(x)-f_j(x)$, $m_{aj} = g_{aj}(x^r) > 0$.
Event set $\mathcal{S}_e(x^r) = \{x^r + D_e u : \|u\|_\infty \le 1\}$ with $D_e = \mathrm{diag}(r^{(e)}_1,\ldots,r^{(e)}_d)$.
Adverse shift $B^*_{aj}(e) = \sup_{x \in \mathcal{S}_e}[m_{aj} - g_{aj}(x)]$; certificate needs $m_{aj} > B^*_{aj}(e)$ $\forall j \ne a$.
\begin{proposition}[Soundness]
If $m_{aj} > B^*_{aj}(e)$ for all $j \ne a$, then $\arg\max_k f_k(x) = a$ for all $x \in \mathcal{S}_e$.
\end{proposition}
CROWN affine lower bound $g_{aj}(x) \ge \alpha_{aj}^\top x + \beta_{aj}$ on $\mathcal{S}_e$ gives
\begin{equation}
\underline{g}^{(e)}_{aj} = \alpha_{aj}^\top x^r + \beta_{aj} - \|D_e^\top\alpha_{aj}\|_1,
\end{equation}
since $\min_{\|u\|_\infty\le 1}\alpha^\top D_e u = -\|D_e^\top\alpha\|_1$.
Report used $\|D_e\alpha\|_1$, valid only because $D_e$ is diagonal symmetric; general $D_e$ needs $D_e^\top\alpha$.
Runtime certificate: $\underline{g}^{(e)}_{aj} > 0$ $\forall j \ne a$.
Robust extension for coverage: $x = x^r + D_e u + v$, $|v_i| \le \epsilon_i$, paying $-\sum_i \epsilon_i|\alpha_i|$.
Diagonal box stays the first baseline; correlated sets after 03\_DATA\_AUDIT confirms data support.
\textbf{Overlap $\ne$ similarity:} 128-window stride 64 shares 64 samples at different CNN coordinates, so $\|x_{t+1}-x_t\|_\infty \approx 0$ does not follow; G1 must measure deltas vs.
 margins.
